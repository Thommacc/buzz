# Multi-Community AI Workforce Design

Date: 2026-08-19
Status: Approved design with Hermes membership extension, pending final review

## Problem

Buzz currently represents each combination of agent and community as a separate
managed-agent record. The same logical employee therefore appears multiple
times with the same name and face. The current rollout is also hard-coded for
three communities, repeats an identical role prompt in every company, and
requires manual code changes when a community is added.

Thom must be able to use one recognizable AI employee in several companies in
the same way that one human can work for several companies: the identity and
general profession remain stable, while company knowledge, permissions,
memory, model policy, and current work remain isolated.

## Goals

- Keep exactly one logical identity, name, avatar, and role prompt per AI
  employee.
- Allow Thom's existing Hermes agents to join any registered community as
  their existing identities, without cloning their profiles or runtimes.
- Allow each identity to be a member of any number of Buzz communities.
- Select company context from the authenticated community boundary rather
  than from text supplied by a user or model.
- Keep company documents, facts, conversations, and learned preferences
  isolated.
- Make all 35 employee roles discoverable without requiring Thom to memorize
  their names.
- Support a default model per role, an optional company-specific override, and
  an explicit task-level override.
- Add Damen and DSRY through data entries rather than source-code changes.
- Preserve owner-only activation and require human approval before proposed
  knowledge becomes trusted company context.
- Avoid restarting or disrupting WSL, Hermes, or healthy existing agents
  during staging.

## Non-Goals

- Automatically trust facts generated by an agent.
- Share conversation memory or private documents between companies.
- Start every employee automatically when a community is added.
- Replace Hermes or move the workforce into Hermes internals.
- Rewrite Hermes agent personalities, tools, or fixed runtime defaults as part
  of community onboarding.
- Automatically add or start every Hermes agent in every community.
- Redesign unrelated Buzz messaging, relay, or mobile behavior.

## Recommended Architecture

The implementation introduces a workforce control plane beside the existing
Buzz and Hermes runtimes. It separates logical employee definitions from
community memberships and runtime connections.

### 1. Employee Catalog

The catalog contains one record for each of the 35 reusable roles:

- stable `role_id`;
- one Buzz identity/public key;
- display name and avatar;
- general role prompt and operating procedure;
- department and plain-language "use when" description;
- required inputs and expected outputs;
- default model policy;
- approval and safety boundaries;
- catalog version and last verified timestamp.

The role prompt never contains company-specific facts.

Hermes agents remain a separate identity class from the 35 employee roles.
They retain their existing profile, name, avatar, tools, model policy, and
runtime ownership. The workforce control plane stores only their stable
identity reference and community memberships; it does not copy or replace a
Hermes profile.

### 2. Community Registry

The registry contains one record per company:

- stable `company_id`;
- Buzz community name and authenticated relay URL;
- company-context reference;
- enabled employee roles and enabled Hermes identities;
- membership and connector status;
- default language and model policy;
- owners and approvers;
- rollout state: `draft`, `canary`, `active`, or `disabled`.

Adding Damen or DSRY creates a registry record and company profile. It does not
copy or edit the 35 role definitions.

### 3. Company Context Package

Each company receives a separately stored and versioned context package:

- company profile, services, products, markets, and customers;
- people, departments, responsibilities, and approval limits;
- tone of voice and supported languages;
- processes and reusable templates;
- approved documents and source references;
- active facts with provenance and review date;
- proposed facts awaiting approval;
- company-specific model overrides;
- company-local memory namespace.

An agent may propose a new fact with its source, timestamp, and confidence. A
proposal becomes active only after approval by Thom or a configured company
approver. Rejected or superseded facts remain auditable but are not injected
into prompts.

### 4. Multi-Community Gateway

One logical employee can hold memberships in multiple communities. The gateway
maintains the required relay connections without exposing each connection as a
separate employee to the user.

The same membership mechanism applies to Hermes agents. Atlas, Nova, Lyra, or
another registered Hermes identity can be enabled in a community while keeping
one public identity and one underlying Hermes profile. Community connectors are
transport bindings, not cloned Hermes agents.

For every inbound request the gateway:

1. verifies the signed event and authenticated relay/community boundary;
2. resolves `company_id` from the registry;
3. resolves the requested role directly or through intake;
4. loads only that company's approved context and memory namespace;
5. resolves the role, company, and task-level model policy;
6. composes the execution prompt;
7. invokes the selected model/runtime;
8. returns the answer to the originating community as the same employee
   identity;
9. records provenance, chosen context version, role version, and model route.

Unknown, disabled, or mismatched communities fail closed. A model cannot switch
companies by mentioning another company name in its output or tool call.

### 5. Prompt Composition

Every execution prompt is built in this fixed order:

1. immutable safety and approval rules;
2. reusable employee role prompt;
3. approved company context for the authenticated community;
4. relevant company-local memory and documents;
5. the current task and explicit user constraints.

The execution record stores hashes or versions for every injected layer. It
does not copy secrets into logs.

### 6. Model Policy

Model resolution follows this precedence:

1. explicit task override chosen by Thom;
2. company-specific override for the selected role;
3. role default from the employee catalog;
4. fail closed when no approved and healthy route exists.

Only currently available, health-checked endpoints may be selected. Model
choice does not change the employee identity or company context. The UI shows a
friendly purpose label together with the actual model name.

### 7. Intake and Role Discovery

Every community has an `AI Team - Intake & Regie` entry point. Thom describes
the desired outcome in ordinary language. Intake returns:

- one recommended primary employee;
- at most two supporting employees when necessary;
- a short reason for each recommendation;
- expected input and output;
- the model that will be used;
- any required approval before execution.

Thom confirms the recommendation before a consequential or external action.
He can also address a known employee directly by name.

The global employee directory shows each of the 35 identities once. Each card
lists its role, department, enabled communities, default model, overrides,
runtime health, and last canary result. Technical relay connectors are not
rendered as employees.

The directory has a separate Hermes section. Each Hermes agent appears once
with its enabled communities and live runtime health. Thom can add or remove an
existing Hermes identity from a community without editing its profile or
creating another visible agent card.

## Data Isolation and Security

- The authenticated relay/community mapping is the tenant boundary.
- Every context, memory, artifact, and execution record is keyed by
  `(company_id, role_id)` or a stricter company-local identifier.
- Cross-company reads are denied before prompt composition.
- Owner-only activation remains the default.
- External, destructive, credential, financial, and production-impacting
  actions require the existing human approval boundary.
- Secrets remain in their existing secret stores and are referenced, not
  embedded in company sheets or prompts.
- Proposed knowledge is untrusted until approved.
- Company-context changes and model overrides are versioned and reversible.

## Failure Handling

- Missing company profile: explain the missing context and do not execute.
- Unknown community: reject without falling back to another company.
- Conflicting facts: retain both as a review item and inject neither as a
  trusted fact.
- Unhealthy preferred model: show the failure and request approval for a known
  fallback unless an approved fallback policy already exists.
- Connector loss: retry the connector without duplicating the employee or
  replaying a completed external action.
- Context update failure: retain the last approved version and mark the new
  proposal as failed.

## Migration and Rollout

1. Read-only snapshot the current 267-record store, receipts, running
   connectors, identities, memberships, relays, and hashes.
2. Build and test the catalog, registry, context packages, and resolver without
   changing live Buzz state.
3. Import the existing 35 AI employee definitions as unique logical identities.
4. Import the existing Hermes identity registry and its current community
   memberships without restarting a healthy Hermes service.
5. Import Thommacc Labs, TotalTools, and Van der Hilst as registry entries while
   preserving their data and permissions.
6. Add draft entries for Damen and DSRY; create or join their communities only
   after exact relay and ownership preflight.
7. Canary one low-risk employee in one existing community, proving identity,
   context isolation, model routing, and reply attribution.
8. Canary the same identity in a second company and prove that no context or
   memory crosses the boundary.
9. Canary one existing Hermes identity in a second community without changing
   or restarting its healthy base runtime.
10. Activate Damen and DSRY one at a time with autostart disabled for all other
   employees.
11. Replace duplicate employee presentation only after rollback evidence and
   cross-community canaries pass.
12. Preserve the previous store and receipts as rollback artifacts until Thom
    accepts the completed rollout.

## Verification Criteria

- The global directory contains 35 logical AI employees, not 35 multiplied by
  the number of companies.
- Every registered Hermes agent appears once, regardless of the number of
  community memberships.
- One test identity has the same public key, name, and avatar in at least two
  communities.
- DSRY and Damen each load their own approved profile automatically.
- A deliberate cross-company context probe is denied.
- Company-local conversation and memory readbacks contain no other company.
- Intake recommends the expected role for representative tasks and explains
  why.
- Role default, company override, and task override model precedence are each
  tested.
- New company onboarding requires registry and context data only, with no
  source-code relay list edit.
- Thom can add a selected existing Hermes identity to Damen or DSRY without
  cloning its profile, changing its base model, or exposing another company's
  memory.
- New communities and employees remain stopped until their canary is approved.
- Existing Buzz, WSL, Hermes, and current healthy agents remain available
  throughout staged verification.

## Accepted Product Decisions

- One AI identity can work in several companies.
- Any existing Hermes identity can be granted membership in any registered
  community while remaining one agent with one profile.
- An employee keeps one name, face, and general profession across communities.
- Company context and memory are automatically selected from the authenticated
  community.
- All 35 roles remain available through direct selection and intake.
- Company information grows through proposed updates with human approval.
- Thom can choose a model through defaults and explicit overrides.
- Damen and DSRY are the first new communities onboarded through the registry.
